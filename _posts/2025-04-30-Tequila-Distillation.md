The "Tequila El Alquimista" project focused on designing a highly efficient, scalable tequila production facility that bridges traditional Mexican heritage with modern chemical engineering practices. The plant was designed to produce a steady-state liquid stream of 29,655.2 lb/hr of tequila (40 wt% ethanol and 60 wt% water) at 106.07 °F.

The technical scope covered the entire production lifecycle, including agave hydrolysis, fermentation, and a two-stage distillation process. The core chemical conversion relied on the fermentation of fructose. The plant's infrastructure was modeled to include an agave oven, three 10,000 L stainless steel fermentation tanks, two 15-stage carbon steel distillation columns, and 100 oak aging barrels, with a total estimated equipment cost of $335,000. 

Beyond process design, the project prioritized sustainability. It incorporated water recycling, carbon footprint reduction strategies, solar panel integration for energy, and the repurposing of fermentation sediment and agave fibers (vinasse and bagasse) as fertilizers for local farms.


Process Simulation & Analysis: Enhanced the fundamental understanding of the tequila distillation process by simulating multiple distillation columns and analyzing key operational parameters. You utilized AVEVA Pro/II and Aspen Plus simulations to guide precise equipment sizing, process flow parameters, and complex heat and material energy balances.

Economic Evaluation & Costing: Researched the production and manufacturing costs for every step of the process. This comprehensive economic modeling resulted in a highly feasible business plan projecting an annual profit of $520,000 and an estimated payback period of roughly 0.6 to 2 years.

Sustainable Systems Design: Championed environmental feasibility by integrating waste management strategies for raw materials and energy recovery systems, ensuring the plant design was both economically and ecologically viable for long-term artisanal or industrial use.
Equipment Specification: Determined operating conditions, material selections (e.g., carbon steel for tray columns, stainless steel for reflux drums), and capacities for over 14 distinct pieces of major plant equipment.
